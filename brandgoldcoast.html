Amruta Jadhav has a habit of asking one more question than people expect: "What does this actually need to do?" A brief might ask for a presentation. She'll ask who needs to use it, what they need to understand, and whether they'll have to rebuild it again next month. More often than not, the answer isn't just a presentation. It's a system.

Across nearly a decade in design, she's worked with advertising agencies and in-house teams, designing for brands including Toyota, JBL, Harman Kardon, Amazon, AstraZeneca, Sterling Holidays and Emma Sleep, without ever settling into one lane.

This is her pitch for the in-house design role at Brand Gold Coast Pty Ltd, told the way the brand tells everyone else's story.

Tram to party

During her first weeks on the Gold Coast, Amruta kept hearing "tram to party" instead of "tram departing."

She never really corrected herself.

It matched the version of the Gold Coast she already had in her mind: beaches, events, visitors and something always happening.

Living here showed her there was much more to the city.

Moving somewhere new was not always a straight line. There were new train and tram routes to understand, unfamiliar places to navigate and the strange feeling of starting again somewhere that did not yet know your story.

That began to change when Amruta started working from Southport Library. Surrounded by creative people, community activity and local events, she began to see the Gold Coast differently. It was not simply a destination. It was a place where people were building businesses, ideas, communities and new versions of themselves.

Hard to put in one box

Across nearly a decade in design, Amruta has never settled into one narrow lane.

Her work has included campaign design, digital experiences, websites, social content, presentations, print, motion and complete visual systems. Sometimes, several of those in the same afternoon.

She has worked with advertising agencies and in-house teams, designing for brands including Toyota, JBL, Harman Kardon, Amazon, AstraZeneca, Sterling Holidays and Emma Sleep.

The variety has never been the difficult part. It is what keeps her curious.

She likes understanding how things work, why they matter and how one idea can remain recognisable wherever people experience it.

"I don't think about design as a collection of separate assets. I think about how one idea moves through a website, a social post, a presentation or a printed piece and still feels like it came from the same place."

More than a deck

She is currently working for Mantra 108.

As part of a growing startup, her role quickly expanded beyond UX and interface design. She contributed across the organisation's digital presence, campaign materials, social content, presentations and printed pieces. Mantra 108's wellness festivals saw a 20% increase in bookings, adding 500 participants, and a 45% increase in weekly participation.

The team was regularly rebuilding the same kinds of materials from scratch. Every presentation, social tile or event asset became another one-off design.

Rather than continuing to create individual pieces, Amruta stepped back and looked at what the team repeatedly needed.

She developed a shared visual language, reusable components and practical templates that could work across different platforms. The result was not just one polished post, brochure or presentation. It was a system the team could continue using without reinventing everything each time, as the startup started looking like an established brand.

It may be less dramatic than one big creative idea, but for a small team, it makes a real difference.

A Gold Coaster, on and off the screen

Amruta's connection to the Gold Coast also extends beyond her design work.

She had the opportunity to model for SkyPoint's Indian wedding-themed campaign, collaborating with several Gold Coast businesses and creative partners for the launch.

It gave her another view of the city's creative community and showed her what can happen when local brands, people and cultures come together to tell one story.

When she is not designing, she is usually exploring the city through its food, places and people.

The concept

Found in Nature

Her speculative concept, Found in Nature, draws colour from the Gold Coast around her: sunrise pinks, ocean blues, warm sand and eucalyptus greens.

Using her own photographs, she turns these naturally occurring colours into digital gradients.

"I wanted the colours to feel like they belonged to a real place," she says. "Not just something selected from a colour picker."

Found in Nature ↗

A gradient generator inspired by colours found in photographs I have taken.

Each photograph becomes a unique gradient, connecting the digital system to the landscape that inspired it. Over time, photos contributed by other Gold Coasters could build an evolving colour library shaped by the people who experience the city every day.

How she'd work

Amruta is comfortable taking a concept end to end: refining type and layout, building reusable Figma systems, preparing production-ready artwork, and adapting one idea across digital, social, print, presentations and physical touchpoints without losing the thread. She also knows the less glamorous side of in-house work well: five requests landing at once, feedback that needs a same-day turnaround, formats that have to stay consistent even when nobody's watching, and the general reality of a small team where everyone pitches in on things outside their job title.

"I like the big idea," she says. "But I care just as much about the small details that let it survive contact with every format it eventually has to work in."

The Gold Coast may first have sounded like "tram to party."

Now, it feels like somewhere Amruta can contribute to a much bigger story: a city known for its beaches and events, but also for its creativity, ambition, community and the people working to build what comes next.
